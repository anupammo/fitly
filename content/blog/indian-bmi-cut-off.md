Most BMI charts online say a BMI under 25 is normal. For Indians, that line sits too high.

## The "thin-fat" Indian body

At the same BMI, South Asians tend to carry more body fat and more fat around the abdomen than Europeans. That fat around the organs is linked to insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes and heart disease. This is why many Indians who look slim are diagnosed with diabetes in their 30s and 40s.

## The Indian cut-offs

Indian consensus guidelines (2009) recommend:

| BMI | Category |
| --- | --- |
| Below 18.5 | Underweight |
| 18.5 – 22.9 | Normal |
| 23 – 24.9 | Overweight |
| 25 and above | Obese |

The WHO also notes that Asian populations face higher risk at lower BMI.

## Check your waist too

BMI does not show where fat sits. A simple tape measure adds a lot:

- **Waist circumference:** above 90 cm for men or 80 cm for women is a warning sign under Indian guidelines.
- **Waist-to-hip ratio:** above 0.90 (men) or 0.85 (women) signals higher risk.

## What to do with your number

1. Calculate your BMI with our [BMI calculator](/tools/bmi-calculator), which uses Indian cut-offs by default.
2. Measure your waist and check your [waist-to-hip ratio](/tools/waist-to-hip-ratio-calculator).
3. If either is high, ask your doctor about fasting blood sugar, HbA1c and a lipid profile.

Small changes add up: a daily 30-minute walk, one fewer fried snack and an extra katori of dal or sabzi instead of a second helping of rice.
