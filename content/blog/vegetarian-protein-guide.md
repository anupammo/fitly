Surveys repeatedly find that most Indian diets fall short on protein, especially vegetarian ones built around rice, roti and sabzi.

## How much you need

| Who | Grams per kg of body weight |
| --- | --- |
| Sedentary adult | 0.8 – 1.0 |
| Exercising regularly | 1.2 – 1.6 |
| Building muscle | 1.6 – 2.2 |
| Age 60+ | 1.0 – 1.2 |

A 60 kg adult who walks daily needs roughly 72–96 g of protein a day. Use the [protein calculator](/tools/protein-calculator) for your own number.

## Protein in everyday Indian foods

Approximate values per serving:

- 1 katori dal — about 8 g
- 100 g paneer — about 18 g
- 100 g firm tofu — about 13 g
- 1 katori curd — about 3 g
- 1 glass toned milk — about 7.5 g
- 1 bowl moong sprouts — about 5 g
- 1 handful roasted peanuts — about 7.5 g
- 1 besan chilla — about 7 g

## A sample day with about 80 g

- **Breakfast:** 2 besan chillas with curd (≈17 g)
- **Lunch:** 2 rotis, 1 katori rajma, salad (≈16 g)
- **Snack:** 1 glass milk and a handful of peanuts (≈15 g)
- **Dinner:** 100 g paneer bhurji, 2 rotis, 1 katori dal (≈33 g)

## Tips

1. Add a protein source to every meal, not just dinner.
2. Pair cereals with pulses (rice with dal, roti with chana) for better protein quality.
3. Check our [food calorie database](/calories) for the protein in any dish.

People with kidney disease should follow their doctor's protein advice.
